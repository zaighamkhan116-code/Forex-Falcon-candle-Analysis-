## Forward-validation procedure
For every newly completed 20-result batch, analyze results before making any further tuning. Record wins, losses, ties, accuracy excluding ties, max loss streak, confidence calibration, direction reranks, and pair/timeframe distribution. Compare against the prior forward baseline and the >52% live accuracy objective.

Read the market state around losses and streaks. Include momentum, structure, FVG state/mitigation/rejection, candle anatomy, liquidity behavior, MTF agreement/conflict, ATR/volatility, VWAP/VSA, tick reliability/density, session/time-of-day, transition/continuation state, and direction-score disagreement.

## Dead / low-volume market rule
Treat dead-market and low-volume periods as a distinct diagnostic regime, not as generic directional failure. Specifically review low ATR/range, low relative/tick volume, low tick rate/density, weak tick reliability, compression, narrow bodies/ranges, low efficiency, poor follow-through, and session/off-hour context. Do not automatically suppress signals because of dead market; first prefer score redistribution, confidence compression, direction calibration, and regime-aware weighting so required frequency remains intact.

## Autonomous tuning authorization
After each newly completed 20-result batch, targeted scoring/fusion/calibration changes may be made without waiting for another user approval when the evidence is clear and the change preserves required signal frequency. Avoid one-pair curve-fitting; validate corrections across active pairs/timeframes. Add or update regression tests for each runtime change and record the commit plus rationale in the research log.

## Reporting requirement
When the user returns, report each completed 20-result batch reviewed, the market state, the diagnosed loss causes, every tuning change made after that batch, commit SHAs, and whether subsequent fresh results improved or worsened. Clearly separate observed forward results from inferred/backtested effects.
